Captions

  1. Scraps records edible food waste. Every input in this walkthrough is fictional, not a household trial.
  2. The separate example totals 2.03 kg. Its emissions and water figures use global production averages.
  3. Beef leads by estimated emissions; milk leads by weight. The example does not overwrite the personal log.
  4. Two fictional entries: 1,000 g of bread in the previous week and 100 g of beef in the latest week.
  5. An unchecked day means missing data, not zero waste. Thirteen completed days are not enough for comparison.
  6. After all 14 days are complete: 90% less weight, but 8.38 kg CO2e higher estimated production emissions.
  7. Food-level changes explain the total: beef adds 9.95 kg CO2e, while bread falls by 1.57 kg CO2e. Values are rounded.
  8. Water tells a different story: bread falls by 648 L and beef rises by 145.1 L, for a net 502.9 L decrease.
  9. The two entries and completion marks survive a reload. These are changes in logs, not proven environmental savings.
  10. Simulated damage: the recording script inserts an invalid saved row, then reloads. Scraps warns and reopens its day.
  11. The comparison stays unavailable until the log is checked and completed again. This video verifies app behaviour, not household impact.

Recorded from the October 30 local production build with Playwright. The script checks displayed results throughout all 11 scenes.
